Client Portfolio Ranking Methodology

Methodology

VentureLens turns a public dataset of Y Combinator startups into a sized, explainable portfolio. Code does every calculation; an LLM only explains, matches comparables and summarises sentiment. It never sets a score or a weight.

1. The client

A growth investor with $100,000, a long horizon and high risk tolerance, who wants to know which startups to back, how much to put in each, and why. The default mandate keeps 10% in cash, holds 10 positions, and limits each position to between 4% and 25%.

On the main page the client can change the capital, cash reserve, number of positions, position cap, sectors and stages. The page re-runs the same allocation maths in the browser. With the default settings it reproduces the pipeline's portfolio to the dollar. Changed settings are kept in the page link, so a mandate can be shared.

Companies with no disclosed funding or no outside backers can't be priced, so they are dropped. 215 startups remain and are scored; 169 of them are still operating and eligible.

3. Score

The dataset has no revenue, valuation or runway figures, so each factor uses the closest observable proxy. Each factor is scaled to 0–1 and combined with fixed weights:

Score = 0.35 × Valuation + 0.25 × Survival + 0.20 × Backing + 0.10 × Sector + 0.10 × Stage
FactorWeightProxy
Valuation35%Upside to peer-implied fair value (see below), min–max scaled between the 5th and 95th percentiles

Stage10%Earlier is better for growth: Seed 1.00, Series A 0.83, Series B 0.67, Series C+ 0.50, Pre-IPO 0.25. Stage comes from total disclosed funding.

4. Valuation proxy

With no revenue to price against, the model uses dollars raised per outside backer as a multiple. It estimates what a company "should" have raised from what similar companies raised per backer:

Observed multiple = total raised ÷ outside backers Peer multiple = median multiple of companies in the same sector and stage (falls back to sector, then stage, then all companies when a peer group has fewer than 10 members) Fair value = outside backers × peer multiple Upside = fair value ÷ total raised − 1

Medians are taken after clipping the top and bottom 1%, so a few extreme rounds can't dominate.

5. Allocation

  1. Keep operating companies that fit the mandate's sectors and stages, and take the top 10 by score.
  2. Tilt towards larger, better-funded companies, which are more de-risked. Log scaling stops a $500M company from getting 1,000× the weight of a $500k one:
    Funding factor = ln(1 + raised in $k) ÷ average across picks Size factor = ln(1 + backers) ÷ average across picks Allocation = score × √(funding factor × size factor)
  3. Scale the weights to fill the invested share (100% minus cash). Then "water-fill": pin any position outside the 4%–25% band at the limit and spread the difference over the others, repeating until everything fits.
  4. Convert to whole dollars. Any rounding remainder goes to the top-scored pick.

If the cap is too low to invest everything (for example 5 positions capped at 10%), the rest stays in cash and the page says so.

6. Public comparables

Startups can't be bought on an exchange, so each pick is matched to listed companies that do similar work. This gives market context; they are not substitutes.

  • Match: an LLM proposes three ranked listed companies, with the rationale, similarities, differences and a confidence score. It prefers established US listings that file with the SEC when matches are equally good. Responses are cached so runs are reproducible.
  • Fetch: the highest-ranked match that has a Finnhub profile and enough annual financials is used. The page shows its market cap, revenue growth, gross margin, 52-week return and annual revenue from SEC filings.

7. Sentiment

For each pick, recent Google News headlines and Hacker News discussions about the company and its founders are collected, and an LLM scores them from −1 to 1:

Overall = 0.6 × company sentiment + 0.4 × founder sentiment (company only when there is no founder coverage)

The result is labelled from Very Bearish to Very Bullish, with a written summary, key risks and growth catalysts. If the LLM is unavailable, a local lexicon model (VADER) is used instead. Sentiment is shown as context only; it does not change the weights.

8. Limitations

  • The startup data covers 2005–2014 YC cohorts; many companies have since changed, been acquired or closed.
  • Every factor is a proxy. Funding per backer is not revenue, and survival is not runway.
  • Comparables and sentiment are only produced for the pipeline's own portfolio. A custom mandate may pick companies that have no comparable or sentiment yet.
  • LLM matches and summaries can be wrong; confidence scores and sources are shown so they can be checked.
